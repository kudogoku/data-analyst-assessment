Retention & Churn Indicators

✅
Low Cancel & Refund Rate
Only 2.0% of orders were cancelled or refunded (1,816 orders). This is a positive signal, but without reliable last_login data it is not possible to confirm whether overall churn is actually low — users may have stopped watching without cancelling.
⚡
Repeat Purchase Cohort
29.6% of buyers made 2 or more purchases (17,577 users). The max purchases per user is 6, and the average across buyers is 1.36. Building loyalty programs for this high-value segment could drive disproportionate revenue uplift.
⚠️
80% last_login Data Missing
52,294 out of 63,138 user records have no last_login value. This makes it impossible to calculate proper churn rate, DAU/MAU, or identify at-risk users. Cancel & refund rate is used as a proxy only — actual churn behaviour is unknown.
06

Data Quality Issues & Anomalies

  • HIGH
    Country codes in userdata.csv and viewdata.csv are inconsistent — "TH", "THA", "Thai", "Thailand" all represent the same country, inflating apparent diversity. After normalization, Thailand accounts for 84.2% of users (not the fragmented picture the raw data shows).
    Impact: Incorrect geographic segmentation. Fix: Standardize to ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 at ingestion.
  • HIGH
    is_active and is_staff columns mix boolean integers (0/1) with text strings ("Y", "yes", "N", "no"). 5 different representations exist for what should be a binary field.
    Impact: Any analysis using these fields raw will be wrong. Fix: Normalize to 0/1 boolean at source or ETL layer.
  • HIGH
    signup_date and date_joined in userdata contain mixed formats — "20/3/2026", "Mar 20 2026", "6/11/2025" are all present alongside MM:SS.s fragments. Additionally, 1,894 records have signup_date = "1/1/2030" which is a likely system placeholder. These users are confirmed real (98.3% have viewing activity) but their actual signup date is unknown.
    Impact: Cohort analysis by signup month is unreliable. Fix: Standardize date format at source and replace placeholder values.
  • MEDIUM
    3,597 purchase records have time_refund_processed = 2024-01-01 00:00:00 despite having status = completed — not refunded. This is a system default value, not a real refund date.
    Impact: Refund timing analysis is unreliable. These values should be treated as null.
  • MEDIUM
    1,913 orders show time_created differing from time_ordered by more than 1 hour. This is likely due to timezone differences between the user's device and the server, or order processing delays in the queue.
    Impact: Order lifecycle timing is unreliable for precise analysis. time_ordered is used as the reference timestamp throughout this report.
  • MEDIUM
    7,164 purchase records (8.0%) have no user_id. These are legitimate paid transactions ($55,247 in revenue) but cannot be linked to specific users, preventing full lifetime-value analysis.
    Impact: LTV calculations are underestimated by ~8%. Cause is unknown — could be a data pipeline gap or deleted accounts.
  • MEDIUM
    1,894 duplicate user_id rows in userdata — same user appears more than once with different id values. This inflates the user count from 63,138 actual unique users to 65,032 rows.
    Fix: Deduplicate on user_id before any user-level aggregation. May indicate account merges or import errors.
  • MEDIUM
    payment_vendor has inconsistent casing: "stripe", "Stripe", "STRIPE" all appear, as do "paypal", "PayPal", "PAYPAL". After normalization: Stripe = 13,936 orders (not 692+669+668 separate entries).
    Fix: Normalize vendor names to title case at ETL layer.
  • MEDIUM
    currency column contains leading/trailing spaces (" THB", "THB ", etc.) creating 153 apparent unique currency codes when only ~20 real currencies exist.
    Fix: Apply .strip() and .upper() at ingestion. Standard data cleaning step that's being missed upstream.
  • LOW
    leave_type in viewdata uses mixed case ("Disconnect" vs "DISCONNECT") and synonyms ("timedout" vs "timeout") creating 7 values for what should be 5 distinct exit reasons.
    Fix: Normalize at event logging level. Minor but creates confusion in dropout analysis.
  • LOW
    19 "Comped" orders exist with $0 revenue and fulfilled status. These represent free/gifted subscriptions given to users. While small in number, they should be tracked separately for accurate revenue vs. access metrics.
    All 19 are 3-Month Pass products. May be influencer/partnership comps.
  • LOW
    user_session_count in viewdata shows a value of 1 for every single record (mean = 1.0, std = 0.0). This field appears to not be populated correctly — it likely should track how many times a user has watched, but is always 1.
    Field is effectively unusable in current state. Likely a logging defect.
07

Actionable Recommendations

01
Fix Upstream Data Quality
Standardize country codes (ISO 3166), boolean fields, vendor names, and currency values at the API/ingestion layer. Implement schema validation to catch these issues before they reach the analytics layer. Current data requires repeated manual cleaning on every analysis.
02
Promote the 3-Month Pass
The 3-Month Pass generates $14.51/order vs $6.82 for the monthly pass — 2.1× more revenue per transaction with fewer support touchpoints. At current order volumes, shifting 20% of monthly buyers to the 3-month plan would add ~$92K in incremental revenue.
03
Restore last_login Tracking
80% of users have no last_login data. Without this, it's impossible to calculate churn rate, DAU/MAU, or identify at-risk users. This is a critical gap — implement server-side login event logging immediately to enable retention analysis.
04
Geographic Diversification
84% revenue concentration in Thailand is a significant risk. Malaysia (3.4%) and international markets (Singapore, UK, Australia) show meaningful but underdeveloped presence. Consider localized content, pricing, or marketing campaigns targeting MY, SG, and diaspora markets.
05
Loyalty Program for Repeat Buyers
29.6% of buyers have purchased 2+ times. These high-LTV users should be segmented for targeted retention campaigns — early access, discounts, or exclusive content. Increasing their purchase frequency from 2 to 3 orders would meaningfully impact total revenue.
06
Investigate 8K Unlinked Purchases
7,164 fulfilled orders lack a user_id, representing $55,247 in revenue that cannot be attributed to any user. This prevents engagement, retention, or LTV analysis for these transactions. The cause is unknown — investigate whether this is a data pipeline gap and implement user linkage where possible.

Assumptions & Methodology Notes

Revenue Definition Used total_charges_usd as the revenue metric (already converted to USD by the platform). Included only orders with status: fulfilled or completed. Excluded refunded, cancelled, and disputed. Note: tax and unit_price columns are in local currency — not used in revenue calculations.
Country Normalization Mapped "thai", "Thailand", "THA" → "TH"; "USA" → "US"; "UK" → "GB". Applied to both userdata (country) and viewdata (geo_country). All codes converted to uppercase ISO 3166-1 alpha-2.
Duplicate Users 1,894 duplicate user_id entries in userdata were removed (kept first occurrence). Assumed duplicate rows represent data import errors, not separate accounts. All user counts use deduplicated dataset.
Boolean Normalization is_active and is_staff: mapped "Y"/"yes"/1 → 1, "N"/"no"/0 → 0. Staff users (is_staff=1) were excluded from active user counts where noted to avoid inflating engagement metrics.
Backdated Refunds 3,597 records with time_refund_processed year < 2026 were flagged as anomalous (likely placeholder "2024-01-01" default). These were not excluded from revenue but are noted as unreliable for timing analysis.
Viewer-Buyer Join Joined viewdata.kiswe_user_id with purchase_data.user_id as the user identifier. Assumed these are the same ID space based on the high overlap observed between the two tables.
Event Revenue Attribution Revenue was not attributed to individual events because all purchases are for "ONE_PASS" (not event-specific tickets). Revenue spikes on event days reflect new subscriptions triggered by events, not per-event ticket sales.
Signup Date signup_date contains mixed formats ("20/3/2026", "Mar 20 2026", MM:SS.s fragments) making consistent parsing unreliable. 1,894 records with signup_date = "1/1/2030" are treated as valid users with unknown signup dates — confirmed by their viewing and purchase activity.
